[Find remote talent today](https://www.roberthalf.com/us/en/hire-talent/remote) **By [Katie Merritt](https://www.roberthalf.com/us/en/insights/authors/katie-merritt), Senior Research and Data Manager, Robert Half**The first 6 months of 2026 have seen a dramatic drop in the number of remote or hybrid job postings. According to a proprietary analysis by Robert Half, the number of fully in-office roles rose from 65% in Q4 2025 to 87% in Q2 2026. This shift suggests that, after several years of announcing changes to remote and hybrid policies, many companies have now implemented their return-to-office plans. In addition, 36% of employers report increasing the number of days employees are required to work on-site over the past year.For employers facing hiring challenges, flexible work arrangements may offer a competitive advantage. Many job seekers continue to prefer hybrid work, and offering even one or two remote days per week could help companies attract skilled candidates. Recent Robert Half researchfound that 46% of professionals are already looking or plan to look for a new role in the second half of 2026. Nearly 2 in 5 (39%) of these job seekers report that finding a job with more remote flexibility is one of their primary motivations.The benefits of hiring remote and hybrid workers are clear for employers. In addition to accessing a wider talent pool and potentially attracting more skilled applicants for jobs, offering flexible work can help improve retention. Among all workers, 64% of workers report that work-life balance and remote work options would motivate them to change jobs.

## Remote and hybrid jobs outlook: On-site roles rise as remote work declines

One of the most significant remote work trends tracked in our research for the [Demand for Skilled Talent](https://www.roberthalf.com/us/en/insights/salary-hiring-trends/demand-for-skilled-talent) report is the continued rise in fully in-office job postings through 2026. As more companies enforce return-to-office policies, on-site roles are becoming increasingly common, reversing some of the earlier gains in workplace flexibility.Of the job postings analyzed in Q2 2026, 87% were fully on-site, while 10% were hybrid and just 3% were fully remote. That’s a step back from the peak of flexible work in previous years, but it also shows that flexibility hasn’t disappeared yet. For job seekers, that means more competition for fully remote roles and a near guarantee that new opportunities will come with at least some in-office time. For employers, offering hybrid arrangements is an opportunity to differentiate themselves and attract candidates who value flexibility.

## Methodology

Surveys were developed by Robert Half and conducted by independent research firms. Respondents included executives from small (1-99 employees), medium (100-999 employees), and large or enterprise (1,000+ employees) businesses in private, publicly listed and public sector organizations across the United States.Job postings provided by TalentNeuron and categorized into more than 450 job titles within [Robert Half’s 2026 Salary Guide](https://www.roberthalf.com/us/en/insights/salary-guide) using a proprietary mapping methodology that employs state-of-the-art large language models. This dataset includes roles across the finance and accounting, technology, marketing and creative, legal, administrative and customer support, human resources and healthcare support professions.
